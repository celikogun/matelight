Insights

Small decisions, big effects.

Short notes from our work on how test design shapes the scores you rely on.

Item writing

Too long to be wrong?

The longest option is often the key.

In weak multiple-choice items the correct answer can be longer or more carefully worded. Test-wise candidates spot it without using the reading skill being measured. That is construct-irrelevant variance.

Test design

Does the order of items matter?

Item and task order can affect performance, anxiety and the overall test-taking experience. There may be no single correct order, but there should be a reason for the one you choose.

Writing assessment

Rating scales have to fit their purpose.

A writing outcome becomes two parts: the task design and the scoring criteria. Analytic criteria help diagnose strengths and weaknesses, and regular monitoring keeps scores reliable.
